About Us

A student-founded response to a question schools weren't asking

Youth AI Literacy Pathways exists to make sure young people don't just use AI — they understand it, question it, and can spot where it falls short of serving them fairly.

Our Mission

To promote equitable access to critical and responsible AI education, so that a student's understanding of AI doesn't depend on where they happen to go to school.

Our Vision

A generation of young people who can engage with AI with both curiosity and a clear eye for bias — equipped to build, question, and shape the technology that will shape them.

What guides us

Our core values

Curiosity

We start from questions, not conclusions — why does this model behave this way, and for whom?

Equity

Every resource we build is free and designed to remove barriers, not assume access to expensive tools.

Integrity

We ground everything in real research, not hype — and we're honest about what we don't yet know.

Youth Leadership

This work is led by a student, for students — the perspective of young people shapes every decision.

Our journey

Why this started, and where it's going

01

The Problem

As AI tools became part of everyday life for students, it became clear that most young people were being taught to use these systems — but almost never taught to question them, or to notice when they don't work equally well for everyone.

02

The Research

Independent research into AI bias among young people confirmed the gap: students had opinions about AI, but rarely the vocabulary or framework to explain why a system might be unfair, or to whom.

03

Where We're Going

Youth AI Literacy Pathways was founded to turn that research into workshops, discussion guides, and partnerships — starting small, and growing with every student and educator who gets involved.

SG
Founder

Sarah Higashio Gocsei is a multilingual high school student with a strong interest in AI literacy, behavioral economics, and information bias. Growing up in a global and multicultural environment, she became interested in these fields after observing how differences in the information people encounter can shape their perspectives and decisions.

She has published a research paper on AI-driven information inequality, worked as a research intern, presented her findings at the Japan Behaviormetric Society, and been selected as a TEDx Youth speaker. Through these experiences, she became increasingly aware that many young people do not have equal access to the knowledge and critical-thinking skills needed to use AI responsibly — which led her to start Youth AI Literacy Pathways (YALPath), an initiative that aims to help young people from diverse backgrounds better understand AI, think critically about misinformation and bias, and engage with emerging technologies responsibly.
